# Irish Stout (Dry Stout)

**Type:** Style (BJCP 15A)
**Source:** Generated for Phase 1 prototype

## Description
Irish Stout is a dark, dry, roasted ale with a coffee-like bitterness and a dry, bittersweet finish. Famously exemplified by Guinness Draught, it is low in carbonation, light in body, and defined by roasted barley rather than heavily roasted malts.

## Typical Ranges
- OG: 1.036–1.050
- FG: 1.007–1.011
- ABV: 4.0–5.0%
- IBU: 25–45
- SRM: 25–40 (very dark)

## Characteristic Ingredients
- Pale malt base with 10–20% roasted barley (unmalted) for the signature dry, coffee-like roast
- Flaked barley or oats for a smooth, low-carbonation texture
- Fuggle, East Kent Goldings, or Target for restrained, earthy bittering
- Clean Irish/British ale yeast (WLP004 / Wyeast 1098 / US-05 as a neutral substitute)
- Low-sulfate, low-carbonate water

## Brewing Notes
A single-infusion mash works; roasted barley can be added to the mash or boil. Nitrogen serving (75/25 gas) gives the classic creamy head. Unlike sweeter stouts, dry stout keeps crystal malt minimal to stay dry.
